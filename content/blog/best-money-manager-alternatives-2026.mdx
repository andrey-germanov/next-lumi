Money Manager by Realbyte has 50M+ installs for good reason: double-entry-style accounts, main and sub-categories, multi-currency, detailed statistics, and no bank login required, across iOS, Android, and PC. It's a workhorse. But people go looking for an alternative for familiar reasons — ads on the free version, a dated interface, entry that means tapping through several fields per transaction, and no smart capture or forecasting. Here's what to switch to while keeping the parts you like.

## What you're keeping vs. upgrading

Money Manager's appeal is thoroughness without bank sync — you own the data and enter it yourself. Keep that. What's usually worth upgrading is the *speed* of entry (AI capture instead of manual fields), a cleaner ad-free interface, and forward-looking features like a month-end forecast that Money Manager doesn't offer.

## The alternatives

### Lumi — the same privacy, far faster entry

[Lumi](/) keeps Money Manager's core bargain — no bank login, data on your device — but replaces multi-field manual entry with two-second capture: say "lunch twelve", scan a receipt in any language, tap the back of your iPhone with [Back Tap](/blog/what-is-back-tap-iphone), or auto-import from Apple Pay. It's ad-free, adds category budgets that warn before overspending, an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending) Money Manager lacks, savings goals, and a unified 150+ currency view. Free tier includes AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr. iPhone and web (no Android) — see [how to log expenses without typing](/blog/how-to-log-expenses-without-typing).

### Monefy — if you want less, not more

If Money Manager feels heavier than you need, Monefy strips tracking to a colourful category wheel and instant tap-to-add, no bank sync. It's the minimalist counterpoint — fewer reports, faster daily use. We compare it in [Lumi vs Monefy](/blog/lumi-vs-monefy).

### Wallet by BudgetBakers — if you're ready for bank sync

If the manual entry itself is the fatigue, Wallet flips to automatic: bank sync across 15,000+ institutions, smart categorisation, spending limits, and family sharing, on iOS, Android, and web. Premium runs ~$2.99–5.49/mo. The trade-off is handing an aggregator your bank access.

### YNAB — if you want budgeting, not just tracking

Money Manager records the past well; YNAB shapes the future with zero-based budgeting (109 dollars a year). Heavier and pricier, but it's the tool if you want a system that changes behaviour. Start with [what zero-based budgeting is](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting).

## The bottom line

Want the same private, manual model but faster and ad-free, with a forecast: Lumi. Want something simpler: Monefy. Ready to let a bank feed do the entry: Wallet. Want a real budgeting system: YNAB. ## FAQ

### Is Money Manager by Realbyte free?

Yes, with ads. A subscription (about $19.99/yr) or a one-time unlock removes ads and adds data sync. It doesn't connect to your bank in most regions — entry is manual. Lumi keeps that no-bank-login model but is ad-free with AI logging.

### What's the fastest alternative for entering expenses?

Anything with smart capture. Lumi logs a transaction in about two seconds via voice, receipt scan, Apple Pay import, or Back Tap — versus tapping through fields. See [best voice expense trackers](/blog/best-voice-expense-tracker-apps-2026).

### Which alternative keeps my data off the cloud?

Lumi stores everything on-device with no account and no bank login, so there's no server copy to leak. Money Manager and Monefy also avoid bank linking. Details in [best expense trackers with no bank login](/blog/best-expense-trackers-no-bank-login-2026).
