The best AI budgeting prompts give the assistant a clear goal, a time range and permission to dig into the details. Below are 35 copy-paste prompts for Claude and ChatGPT, grouped by what you're trying to do — from a quick spending audit to a full savings plan. They work best when the AI is connected to your actual expenses through a tracker like [Lumi's MCP connector](/blog/lumi-mcp-connect-budget-to-ai), so it answers with your numbers instead of averages.

## Before you start: give the AI your data

A prompt like "help me save money" gets generic tips if the AI can't see your spending. You have three options:

1. **Connect a tracker** — the AI reads your categorized data on demand. Setup guides: [Claude](/blog/how-to-connect-claude-to-your-budget), [ChatGPT](/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-for-budgeting).
2. **Upload an export** — a CSV from your expense tracker (cleaner than a raw bank statement).
3. **Type a summary** — income, fixed costs and rough category totals. Fine for what-ifs, weak for audits.

The prompts below assume option 1. If you're using an export, replace "my Lumi data" with "the attached file".

## How to write a good budgeting prompt

| Do | Why |
|----|-----|
| Name a time range ("last 3 months") | Keeps one odd month from skewing the answer |
| Ask for specific categories and amounts | Forces concrete advice instead of "cut back on extras" |
| State a target ("save $500/month", "15% more") | Gives the AI something to optimize toward |
| Mention constraints ("rent is fixed", "I won't cut the gym") | Avoids suggestions you'll ignore |
| Ask follow-ups | Connected assistants can keep pulling data — "show me the transactions behind that" |

## Spending audit prompts

1. "Using my Lumi data, give me an honest overview of my finances for the last three months: income, spending, savings rate and anything unusual."
2. "What are my five biggest spending categories over the last six months, and which ones are growing?"
3. "Where does my money go by merchant? List my top 15 merchants by total spent and how often I pay them."
4. "Compare this month so far with my average month. Am I spending faster than usual, and on what?"
5. "Why was last month more expensive than the month before? Show me the transactions that explain the difference."
6. "How much do I spend on small purchases under $15? Is it adding up to something significant?" — a quick way to test the [latte factor](/blog/is-the-latte-factor-real) on your own data.
7. "Which categories have the most variance month to month? Those are probably where I'm least in control."
8. "Find any spending that looks like [impulse buying](/blog/what-is-impulse-spending) — late-night purchases, repeated small orders, spikes on weekends."

## Savings plan prompts

9. "How can I save 15% more each month? Work out the target amount from my income and show where it can come from, with specific categories and amounts."
10. "I want to save an extra $500 a month without touching rent or groceries. What's the most realistic way?"
11. "Rank my spending categories by how easy they'd be to cut, and estimate how much I'd save by trimming each by 20%."
12. "If I'd spent like my cheapest month every month this year, how much more would I have saved?"
13. "Suggest a [no-spend challenge](/blog/what-is-a-no-spend-challenge) for next month based on the categories where I overspend most."
14. "How much of my income goes to needs, wants and savings? Compare it with the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/what-is-the-50-30-20-rule)."
15. "What would my savings rate be if I cut food delivery in half?"

## Subscription and recurring payment prompts

16. "List all my recurring payments and anything that looks like a subscription I haven't set up as recurring."
17. "Which subscriptions have I been paying for the longest? Total what each has cost me so far."
18. "How much do my recurring payments add up to per month and per year?"
19. "Are any of my bills going up? Compare recent charges from the same merchants with earlier ones."
20. "Which subscriptions overlap — for example, multiple streaming or cloud storage services?"

## Budget prompts

21. "Build me a realistic monthly budget for next month based on what I actually spent over the last three months."
22. "Compare my current budgets with my real spending. Which limits are unrealistic, too loose or about right?"
23. "Which budgets am I on track to exceed this month, given how much of the month has passed?"
24. "Create a [zero-based budget](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting) from my average income and expenses."
25. "My income varies. Using my history, suggest a baseline budget that works in my lowest-earning months." (More on this in [budgeting with variable income](/blog/how-to-budget-with-variable-income).)
26. "Suggest category budgets that would get me to a 20% savings rate, changing as little as possible."

## Goals and planning prompts

27. "Am I on track for each of my savings goals? For any that are behind, how much more do I need per month?"
28. "When will I reach my emergency fund goal at my current pace? What if I add $150 a month?" See also: [what an emergency fund is and how big it should be](/blog/what-is-an-emergency-fund).
29. "Can I afford a $1,200 trip in March without dipping into savings? Show the math."
30. "What's my average monthly surplus over the last six months, and what's a safe amount to put into savings automatically?"
31. "If my rent goes up $200, what's the least painful way to rebalance my budget?"

## Habits and big-picture prompts

32. "On which days of the week and times of the month do I spend the most? What does that say about my habits?"
33. "Summarize my spending this year in five sentences, like a friend who's honest but kind."
34. "What's one change that would make the biggest difference to my finances, based only on my data?"
35. "Compare my spending this year with last year by category. What changed, and was it intentional?"

## Tips for better answers

- **Ask the AI to show its sources.** "Which transactions is that based on?" — a connected assistant can list them.
- **Set the currency and time zone** if you travel or use several currencies: "Show everything in EUR, I'm in Europe/Berlin."
- **Push back.** If a suggestion doesn't fit your life, say so and ask for alternatives. The second answer is usually better.
- **Act on one thing.** Pick the single biggest recommendation, set it up in your budget app today, and check back next month with prompt 4.

## FAQ

**Can ChatGPT or Claude make me a budget?**
Yes. With access to your real spending, both can build a detailed monthly budget, suggest category limits and run what-if scenarios. Without your data, they can only give generic templates.

**Which AI is better for budgeting, Claude or ChatGPT?**
Both handle these prompts well. The bigger factor is whether the AI can see your data — both support MCP connectors like Lumi's. Claude's free plan includes one custom connector, which makes it the cheapest way to start.

**Is it safe to use AI for budgeting?**
Use a read-only connection where you can, never share bank passwords in a chat, and check your AI provider's privacy settings. Treat the AI as an analyst, not a licensed financial advisor, for decisions about taxes, debt or investing.

**Do these prompts work without a connected app?**
Yes, if you attach a spending export or paste a summary. They're most accurate when the AI can read categorized transactions directly.
