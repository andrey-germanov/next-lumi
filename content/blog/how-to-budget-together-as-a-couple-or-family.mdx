Merge nothing, hide nothing, argue about nothing. This guide shows how to run one shared budget the entire family can access — and how [Lumi](/) makes "who added this?" answerable at a glance.

## The real problem with budgeting together isn't money — it's visibility

Couples and families rarely fight about the total. They fight because one person doesn't know what the other spent until the balance looks wrong. The fix isn't a joint account or a spreadsheet nobody updates — it's a single shared budget where every purchase is visible and attributed, in real time, to the person who made it.

That's the whole job of a shared budget: one place the whole family opens, sees the same numbers, and instantly knows who bought what and from which account. Get that right and the monthly "where did the money go?" conversation disappears.

## Set up a shared family budget in five minutes

1. **Agree who's in.** Couple, spouses, siblings, roommates, or the whole family — decide who shares the budget and add each person by name.
2. **Decide what's shared vs. personal.** Rent, groceries, utilities, kids, joint travel are shared; personal subscriptions and hobbies stay personal. A short shared list is easier to keep honest — see [what a shared budget is](/blog/what-is-a-shared-budget).
3. **Pick your split.** Equal when incomes are similar, proportional to income when they're not. The [couple expense split calculator](/tools/couple-expense-split-calculator) shows both side by side; run the [50/30/20 calculator](/tools/50-30-20-budget-calculator) first so everyone sees how much room they have.
4. **Set shared category limits.** Give groceries, dining, and household a monthly ceiling so the app warns the family before anyone overspends, not after.
5. **Log as you go, review together monthly.** Because every entry is named and attributed, the monthly review is fifteen minutes of reading shared totals — not an interrogation.

## Agree on these before you share a budget

A shared app removes the accounting friction, but the agreement is still yours to make. Settle four things up front: which expenses are shared, how you split them, one shared savings goal funded first like a bill, and a simple rule for big one-off purchases (for example, "anything over €100 for the home is shared unless we say otherwise").
