**Lumi and Money Manager both track spending without a bank login, but they aim at different users: Money Manager is a thorough, ledger-style app that spans phone and desktop, while Lumi is an iPhone-first tracker that removes as much typing as possible with voice, Apple Pay auto-import, and receipt scanning.** If you want a detailed double-entry ledger across devices, Money Manager is built for that. If you want tracking that survives busy weeks because logging barely takes effort, Lumi is built for that.

## Quick comparison

| | Lumi | Money Manager |
|---|------|---------------|
| Entry methods | Voice, Apple Pay auto-import, receipt scan, manual | Manual entry with a built-in calculator |
| Style | Fast capture, category budgets | Detailed ledger (income, expense, assets) |
| AI | Voice parsing, receipt scanning, month-end forecast | None |
| Bank login | No | No |
| Where data lives | On your device | On device, with backup/export options |
| Platforms | iOS | iOS, Android, and desktop |
| Multi-currency | 150+ with live conversion | Supported |
| Price | Free / $4.99/mo ($39.99/yr) | Free version with limits; paid unlock |

## Where Money Manager wins

Money Manager has earned a large, loyal following for good reasons. It offers a genuine double-entry approach that tracks not just spending but accounts and assets, calendar and statistics views for reviewing history in depth, and versions across Android and desktop, so you can enter and review on a big screen. If you like a complete personal ledger and want cross-platform coverage, it is a strong, proven choice.

## Where Lumi wins

The step where tracking habits usually die is entry, and a detailed ledger asks more of you at exactly that step. Lumi attacks the friction directly: say a purchase out loud and AI fills the amount, merchant, and category; contactless payments [log themselves through an Apple Pay Shortcut](/blog/how-to-track-apple-pay-spending); paper receipts scan in a couple of seconds. Category budgets warn you *before* you overspend, and an [AI forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending) projects where your month will land. The point is not a prettier ledger, it is how little work is left to do.

## Which one should you pick?

Choose Money Manager if you are on Android or desktop, or if you want a detailed ledger that tracks assets and accounts, not just expenses. Choose Lumi if you are on iPhone and want tracking to keep going during a hectic week because capturing a purchase takes about two seconds. Both keep your bank credentials out of it, which puts either ahead of sync-based apps on privacy; see [the best expense trackers with no bank login](/blog/best-expense-trackers-no-bank-login-2026) for the wider landscape.

## FAQ

### Does Money Manager connect to my bank?

No. Money Manager is a manual tracker, so you enter transactions yourself. That is the shared philosophy with Lumi; Lumi simply adds automation (voice, Apple Pay import, receipt scan) without touching bank credentials.

### Is Money Manager free?

There is a free version with some limits, and a paid unlock removes them. Lumi is free to download, with an optional, skippable premium tier at $4.99/mo.

### Which is better on iPhone?

If you want the fastest possible logging and an AI forecast, Lumi is designed for that on iOS. If you specifically want a detailed ledger and desktop access, Money Manager is the better fit.

### Which is better for travel?

Lumi handles 150-plus currencies with real-time conversion, and voice or receipt capture keeps working across [multiple currencies on the road](/blog/how-to-track-multiple-currencies).
