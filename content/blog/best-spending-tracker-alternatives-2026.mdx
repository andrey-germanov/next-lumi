The Spending Tracker app by MH Riley has been a quiet staple for over a decade: free, simple, weekly/monthly/yearly views, a budget mode, and clean charts, with in-app purchases for extras like repeat transactions and CSV export. It works — but people outgrow it. The interface feels dated, entry is fully manual with no smart capture, sync is limited to Dropbox backup, and it stops short of real budgeting features like forecasts or savings goals. Here's what to move to when you want more without giving up the simplicity.

## What to keep, what to upgrade

Spending Tracker gets one thing very right: it's fast to grasp and doesn't nag you to link a bank. Keep that. What's worth upgrading is the *speed* of manual entry (AI capture instead of typing every field), smarter charts and forecasts, and proper category budgets that warn you before you overspend rather than after.

## The alternatives

### Lumi — Spending Tracker's simplicity, but entry takes two seconds

[Lumi](/) keeps the no-bank-login, log-it-yourself philosophy that makes Spending Tracker easy — then removes the typing. Say "coffee five", scan a receipt in any language, tap the back of your iPhone with [Back Tap](/blog/what-is-back-tap-iphone), or auto-import from Apple Pay, and the AI fills in amount, merchant, and category. On top of the charts you'd expect, you get budgets that warn before overspending, an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending), savings goals, and a unified 150+ currency view. Free tier includes AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr. iPhone and web (no Android). See [how to log expenses without typing](/blog/how-to-log-expenses-without-typing).

### Money Manager (Realbyte) — more depth, same manual model

If you loved Spending Tracker's reports and want more of them, Money Manager adds main/sub-categories, double-entry-style accounts, multi-currency, and richer statistics — free with ads or $19.99/yr, on iOS, Android, and PC. A natural step up that keeps everything manual and private.

### Monefy — if you want to stay minimalist

Monefy is the modern take on tap-to-add simplicity: a big colourful category wheel, instant entry, and clean summaries, without bank sync. Good if your only complaint about Spending Tracker is the dated look, not the feature set. We compare it directly in [Lumi vs Monefy](/blog/lumi-vs-monefy).

### YNAB — if simple tracking stopped changing anything

Sometimes the issue isn't the app, it's that tracking alone doesn't fix overspending. YNAB's zero-based method (109 dollars a year) makes you assign every dollar a job up front. Heavier, but it's the tool when awareness needs to become a system — start with [why tracking your spending matters](/blog/why-track-your-spending).
