EveryDollar, Ramsey Solutions' zero-based budgeting app, got a big relaunch in January 2026 — a more guided, coaching-style experience with a new Margin Finder tool and paycheck planning. It's a solid way to give every dollar a job. But people look for alternatives for clear reasons: the free tier is manual-only (no bank sync), Premium climbed to $79.99/yr (or $17.99/mo), and the zero-based method plus Ramsey framing isn't for everyone. Here's what to use instead, depending on what you want to keep.

## Decide what you're actually keeping

EveryDollar bundles two things: a *method* (zero-based budgeting) and a *product* (Ramsey's app and coaching). You can keep the method with a cheaper or more flexible app, or drop the method entirely for a lighter tracker. Sort that first — it decides your whole shortlist. If you're new to it, read [what zero-based budgeting is](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting).

## The alternatives

### Lumi — if the manual free tier was the sticking point

EveryDollar's free plan makes you type every transaction; [Lumi](/) is free-tier friendly *and* makes that entry take about two seconds — say "groceries forty", scan a receipt, tap the back of your iPhone, or auto-import from Apple Pay. Instead of strict zero-based envelopes, Lumi uses flexible category budgets that warn before you overspend, plus an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending) that plays a similar role to Margin Finder without a bank login. Everything stays on your device. Free tier includes AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr — far below EveryDollar's $79.99/yr. iPhone and web (no Android), and it's a tracker, not a strict zero-based system — so it's the pick if the method felt rigid.

### YNAB — if you want zero-based done right

If you loved the method and just want the best execution of it, YNAB is the gold standard: rule-based zero-based budgeting, strong education, web and both mobile platforms, at 109 dollars a year. Pricier than EveryDollar Premium, but many find it more powerful. Compare in [best YNAB alternatives](/blog/best-ynab-alternatives-2026) and [is YNAB worth it](/blog/is-ynab-worth-it-2026).

### Goodbudget — zero-based on a budget, literally

Goodbudget brings the envelope version of zero-based to a free tier, with Premium at $10/mo or $80/yr. No bank login — you fund envelopes manually, which keeps the intentionality EveryDollar users like. Good middle ground on price and method.

### Money Manager (Realbyte) — if you're dropping the method

Want to stop budgeting-by-plan and just track cleanly? Money Manager gives you categories, multi-currency, and detailed stats free with ads or $19.99/yr, no bank connection required.

## The bottom line

Loved the method, want the best version: YNAB. Loved the method, want it cheap and private: Goodbudget. Found zero-based too rigid and want fast, flexible tracking without a bank login: Lumi. Just want simple manual tracking: Money Manager. Ground yourself first with [how to budget](/blog/how-to-budget) and the [50/30/20 calculator](/tools/50-30-20-budget-calculator).

## FAQ

### Is EveryDollar still free?

There's a free tier, but it's manual — no bank sync. Premium ($79.99/yr or $17.99/mo, with a 14-day trial) adds bank sync, Margin Finder, paycheck planning, and coaching. If you want AI-assisted logging on a free tier, Lumi includes it without linking a bank.

### What's the best zero-based alternative to EveryDollar?

YNAB for the full rule-based system, or Goodbudget for a cheaper envelope take. Both keep the "every dollar has a job" principle EveryDollar is built on. See [what zero-based budgeting is](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting).

### I found zero-based budgeting too strict — what should I use?

A flexible tracker with category limits instead of rigid envelopes. Lumi lets you set soft budgets that warn before overspending and forecasts your month-end, without the ceremony of assigning every dollar up front.
