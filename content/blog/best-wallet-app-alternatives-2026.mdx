Wallet by BudgetBakers is one of the most capable cross-platform trackers around: bank sync across 15,000+ institutions, smart categorisation, spending limits, family sharing, and strong multi-currency support on iOS, Android, and web. So why do people look for an alternative? The best features sit behind Premium, the free tier is limited to a single wallet, bank connections rely on aggregators that occasionally break or skip a region, and some users simply don't want to hand their bank credentials to a third party. Here's what to use instead, grouped by what you need Wallet for.

## What Wallet does — and where people jump ship

Wallet's strengths are automation and breadth: connect everything, let it categorise, review across accounts and currencies. The friction points are just as predictable — a subscription for the sync that's the whole selling point, occasional broken bank feeds, and the privacy cost of aggregator access. If any of those pushed you here, the alternatives split into "better sync provider" and "skip sync entirely."

## The alternatives

### Lumi — if you want the multi-currency tracking without the bank login

Wallet's multi-currency handling is a big reason travellers and expats use it — and that's exactly [Lumi](/)'s home turf, minus the aggregator. Lumi supports a unified [150+ currency view](/blog/how-to-track-multiple-currencies) with live rates, but you log spending in about two seconds (voice, receipt scan, Apple Pay import, or Back Tap) and everything stays on your device with no bank connection to break. Add category budgets that warn before overspending and an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending). Free tier includes AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr. iPhone and web (no Android) — the honest limits are in [best expense trackers with no bank login](/blog/best-expense-trackers-no-bank-login-2026).

### Monarch Money — if you want an even deeper dashboard

If Wallet felt almost-but-not-quite comprehensive, Monarch is the deep end: full budgets, net worth, investment tracking, and couple access at $14.99/mo or $99.99/yr, with bank sync at the core. It's the closest thing to a complete financial command centre. Full breakdown in [best Monarch Money alternatives](/blog/best-monarch-money-alternatives-2026) if you want to compare the tier.

### Money Manager (Realbyte) — if you want Wallet's reports without a subscription for sync

Money Manager gives you the categorised, report-heavy experience with main/sub-categories, multi-currency, and detailed statistics — free with ads or $19.99/yr — and no bank connection required. Runs on iOS, Android, and PC.

### YNAB — if the real goal is spending less

Wallet shows you where money went; YNAB changes where it goes next. Its zero-based method (109 dollars a year) assigns every dollar a job before you spend. Bank sync optional. Read [what zero-based budgeting is](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting) first.

## The bottom line

Want the multi-currency tracking but private and fast: Lumi. Want a deeper synced dashboard: Monarch. Want the reports without paying for sync: Money Manager. Want to actually cut spending: YNAB. ## FAQ

### Is Wallet by BudgetBakers free?

There's a free tier limited to one wallet. Bank sync — the feature Wallet is known for — is a Premium subscription (roughly $2.99–5.49/mo). If you want AI logging on a free tier without linking a bank, Lumi includes AI scans and voice for free.

### What's the most private Wallet alternative?

A tracker that never connects to your bank. Lumi keeps all data on-device with no aggregator holding your credentials — you log by voice, receipt scan, Apple Pay import, or Back Tap. See [best expense trackers with no bank login](/blog/best-expense-trackers-no-bank-login-2026).

### Which alternative is best for expats and travellers?

Look for a unified multi-currency view with live rates rather than one wallet per currency. Lumi and Money Manager both handle this without bank sync; our [multi-currency roundup](/blog/best-multi-currency-expense-tracker-2026) compares the field.
