Buddy: Budget Planner won a loyal following with a friendly design, custom categories, and genuinely good shared budgets — invite someone, sync transactions, split who paid what, and settle up. But people go looking for an alternative for a few reasons: Premium is on the pricey side ($9.99/mo or $49.99/yr), there's no bank linking so every transaction is manual anyway, and it's iPhone/iPad only. Here's what to switch to, depending on why you're leaving.

## Figure out which Buddy you're replacing

Buddy gets used two very different ways: as a solo tracker with nice categories, or as a couple's shared budget. The best alternative is completely different for each. And if manual entry is the friction point, the fix isn't automation via bank sync — it's logging that's fast enough to not feel like a chore.

## The alternatives

### Lumi — if manual entry was the pain point

Buddy is manual-only, and so is [Lumi](/) — but Lumi's entire design is about making that manual step take about two seconds. Say "lunch twelve", scan a receipt, tap the back of your iPhone with [Back Tap](/blog/what-is-back-tap-iphone), or auto-import from Apple Pay via a Shortcut, and the AI categorises it. You also get budgets that warn before overspending, an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending), savings goals, and a unified 150+ currency view — and it stays on your device with no login. Free tier includes AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr (cheaper than Buddy's $49.99/yr). Best fit if you track your own money; iPhone and web (no Android). More on [logging without typing](/blog/how-to-log-expenses-without-typing).

### Spendee — if you want shared wallets across platforms

If Buddy's shared feature is the draw but you need Android too, Spendee's shared wallets sync in real time, with multi-currency and an AI receipt scanner. Plus is $14.99/yr; bank sync needs Premium. It's a close analogue to Buddy's household model with wider platform support.

### YNAB — for couples who want a real budgeting system

Buddy tracks a shared budget; YNAB *runs* one. Its zero-based method (109 dollars a year) gives every dollar a job, works across web and both mobile platforms, and handles two people sharing one plan. Heavier to learn, but it's the tool if the goal is changing behaviour, not just recording it — see [how to budget as a couple](/blog/how-to-budget-as-a-couple-separate-accounts).

### Money Manager (Realbyte) — the budget option

If Buddy's price is the issue and you mostly track solo, Money Manager covers manual entry, categories, and detailed stats for free with ads or $19.99/yr, on iOS, Android, and PC.

## The bottom line

Track your own spending and want it fast and private: Lumi. Need shared wallets with Android support: Spendee. Want a serious shared budgeting system: YNAB. Want cheap, thorough manual tracking: Money Manager. If you and a partner keep separate accounts, note that private tracking with a [monthly settle-up](/blog/how-to-budget-as-a-couple-separate-accounts) often beats a shared database entirely.

## FAQ

### Why is Buddy manual only?

By design — Buddy doesn't link bank accounts or cards, so you enter each transaction yourself. That's more private than bank sync, but it means logging speed matters. Apps like Lumi keep the no-bank-login privacy while cutting entry to about two seconds.

### Is there a cheaper alternative to Buddy Premium?

Yes. Buddy Premium is $49.99/yr; Lumi Premium is $39.99/yr and Money Manager is $19.99/yr. All three avoid bank linking, so you're not paying more for less privacy.

### What's the best Buddy alternative for couples?

If you truly share one pot, Spendee's shared wallets or YNAB's shared plan fit best. If you keep separate finances, private tracking with a monthly settle-up — the model Lumi uses — is usually cleaner. See [what a shared budget is](/blog/what-is-a-shared-budget).
