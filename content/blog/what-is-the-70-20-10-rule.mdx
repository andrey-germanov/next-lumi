The **70/20/10 rule** is a budgeting method that splits your after-tax income into three simple buckets: 70% for living expenses, 20% for savings and investments, and 10% for debt repayment or giving. It is a looser, easier-to-follow cousin of the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/what-is-the-50-30-20-rule), designed for people who want structure without tracking dozens of categories.

## How the 70/20/10 rule works

Take your monthly income after tax, then divide it by the three percentages.

- **70% — living expenses.** Everything you spend to live: rent or mortgage, groceries, transport, utilities, and the fun stuff too. Unlike the 50/30/20 rule, this version does not force you to separate needs from wants, which is why many people find it simpler.
- **20% — savings and investing.** Your emergency fund, retirement, and any longer-term goals. Paying your future self before the money disappears is the heart of any working budget.
- **10% — debt or giving.** Extra debt payments beyond the minimums, or charitable giving, depending on your priorities.

On a $4,000 monthly take-home, that is $2,800 to live on, $800 to save, and $400 toward debt or giving.

## Who the 70/20/10 rule suits

This split works best if:

- You want a budget you can remember without a spreadsheet.
- Your needs and wants blur together and separating them feels like busywork.
- You already cover essentials comfortably and mainly want a savings target.

It is a weaker fit if your living costs are very high relative to income, in which case 70% may not stretch, or if you are aggressively paying down debt and want a larger slice than 10%.

## 70/20/10 vs 50/30/20

Both are percentage budgets; the difference is how much detail they ask for. The [50/30/20 rule](/blog/what-is-the-50-30-20-rule) splits spending into needs (50%), wants (30%), and savings (20%), which gives more insight but asks you to categorize every purchase. The 70/20/10 rule collapses needs and wants into one 70% bucket, trading some insight for simplicity. If you are new to budgeting, 70/20/10 is often easier to stick with; if you want to see exactly where money leaks, 50/30/20 or [zero-based budgeting](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting) will tell you more.

## How to start

1. **Find your after-tax monthly income.** Use your real take-home pay, not your gross salary.
2. **Set the three targets.** Multiply by 0.70, 0.20, and 0.10.
3. **Automate the 20%.** Move savings on payday so it is gone before you can spend it.
4. **Track your 70%.** The one number that makes or breaks this budget is whether living expenses actually stay near 70%. You only know that if you track your spending.
