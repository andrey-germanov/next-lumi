A developer invited you to test their Android app, you tapped the link, and the Play Store says the app isn't available. That's the most common way a closed beta starts, and it's almost always fixable in a minute. Here's how to join a closed beta on Google Play, why the invite is tied to one specific Google account, and what to check when the beta version doesn't appear.

## What a closed beta on Google Play is

A closed beta is a version of an app that only people on the developer's tester list can install. Google Play calls it **closed testing**. The developer adds testers by Google account email (or through a Google Group), then shares an opt-in link. Nobody outside the list can see or download that build.

There are three kinds of Play testing, and the difference matters when something goes wrong:

- **Internal testing** — a small group, usually the team itself. Builds arrive within minutes.
- **Closed testing** — an invite-only list. This is what most "join our beta" pages lead to.
- **Open testing** — anyone can join, often straight from the app's Play Store page under "Join the beta".

If you were asked for your email first, it's a closed test.

## Step by step: how to join

1. **Give the developer the right email.** It has to be the Google account your phone uses for the Play Store. To check, open the Play Store and tap your profile picture in the top-right corner — the address shown there is the one that counts.
2. **Wait for the invite link.** Google doesn't email testers on its own when they're added to a list, so the developer sends the link themselves. It usually looks like `play.google.com/apps/testing/` followed by the app's ID.
3. **Open the link while signed in to that same account.** On a phone it opens in the browser; on a computer it works too, as long as the browser is signed in to the same Google account.
4. **Tap "Become a tester".** The page confirms that you're now a tester.
5. **Install from the Play Store.** Use the "download it on Google Play" link on that page, or search for the app in the Play Store. It installs and updates like any other app.

The whole thing takes about two minutes once the developer has added your account.

## Beta app not showing up? Check these

Most problems come down to one of a few causes.

### Wrong Google account

This is the cause in the majority of cases. If your phone has several Google accounts, the Play Store may be using a different one than the email you gave. Tap your profile picture in the Play Store, switch to the account that was invited, and open the link again.

### You haven't been added yet

Developers often add testers in batches. If the opt-in page says the link isn't valid for your account, your email probably isn't on the list yet. Reply to the developer with the exact address you signed up with.

### It just needs a little time

After you tap "Become a tester", the Play Store can take from a few minutes to a few hours to show the beta build. Close and reopen the Play Store, or try again later.

### Country or device restrictions

A developer can limit a test to certain countries or devices. If the app is marked as not available in your country or not compatible with your device, only the developer can change that.

### Work or school account

Accounts managed by an employer or school can block testing programs. Use a personal Google account instead.

## What changes once you're a tester

You get the beta version instead of the public one, and updates arrive automatically. If you already had the public version of the app, the beta replaces it — it's the same app, just a newer build.

Expect rough edges. That's the point: the developer wants to find bugs before everyone else does. Most betas have a feedback channel — an email, a chat group, or the "private feedback" section that the Play Store shows testers on the app's page. Specific reports help the most: what you tapped, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot is worth a paragraph.

## Why developers ask you to keep the app for 14 days

New developer accounts on Google Play have to run a closed test before their app can go public. For personal accounts created after November 2023, that means at least **12 testers who stay opted in for 14 days in a row**. If testers leave early, the count drops below the line and the developer has to wait longer.

So if a small developer asks you to keep their beta installed for two weeks, it isn't busywork. Uninstalling on day three can quietly delay their launch.

## How to leave a beta

Open the same invite link and tap **"Leave the program"**. You'll go back to the public version at the next update. If the app isn't public yet, uninstall it. You can usually rejoin later through the same link, as long as you're still on the tester list.

## FAQ

### Is joining a Google Play beta free?

Joining a testing program doesn't cost anything. The app itself follows the developer's usual pricing, so a paid app or in-app purchases can still apply during the beta.

### Do I need a Gmail address to become a beta tester?

You need a Google account, not necessarily a Gmail address. Any email registered as a Google account works, as long as it's the account signed in to the Play Store on your phone.

### Will I lose my data if I join or leave a beta?

Usually not. The beta replaces the app with a newer build of the same app, so local data normally stays. Moving back to an older public version can occasionally cause problems, so back up anything important before you leave.

### Why does the invite link say "app not available"?

Most often the Play Store is signed in to a different Google account than the one the developer added. Switch accounts in the Play Store and open the link again. If that doesn't help, ask the developer to confirm your email is on the tester list.

### Can I join a beta from my computer?

Yes. Open the invite link in a browser signed in to the same Google account and tap "Become a tester". The app then appears in the Play Store on your phone.
