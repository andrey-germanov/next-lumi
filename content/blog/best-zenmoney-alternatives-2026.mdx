Zenmoney has been a favourite of spreadsheet-minded budgeters for years: connect 3,300+ banks, let it auto-import and categorise every transaction, and read detailed income-and-expense reports. But people look for an alternative for a few consistent reasons — the best features (bank sync, planning, extra reports) sit behind the paid Full Zen plan, bank connections in some regions break or stop syncing, and handing an aggregator access to every account is more exposure than some people want. Here's what to use instead, grouped by what you actually need Zenmoney for.

## What Zenmoney is really good at — and where it slips

Zenmoney does three jobs well: automatic bank import, categorised reports, and cross-account planning. The slips are just as consistent. Bank connections depend on third-party aggregators, so a rebuild on the bank's side can silently break your feed. Full Zen is a subscription (monthly, yearly, or lifetime), so the "free" app is mostly manual anyway. And if you're outside its strongest banking regions, the auto-import you signed up for may not cover your accounts. Once you're entering transactions by hand, the question changes: do you want a faster manual tracker, or a different sync provider?

## The alternatives

### Lumi — if you want awareness without the bank login

[Lumi](/) takes the opposite bet from Zenmoney: no bank connection at all, nothing to break, nothing for an aggregator to hold. You log spending in about two seconds — speak "groceries forty", scan a receipt in any language, tap the back of your iPhone, or auto-import from Apple Pay via a Shortcut — and the AI files it. Around that you get category budgets that warn before you overspend, an [AI month-end forecast](/blog/how-to-stop-overspending), savings goals, and a unified [150+ currency view](/blog/how-to-track-multiple-currencies) that suits expats and cross-border households. Free to start with real AI features; Premium is $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr. The trade-off is honest: iPhone and web (no Android), and logging is a small daily habit — see [why a no-bank-login tracker sticks](/blog/best-expense-trackers-no-bank-login-2026).

### Wallet by BudgetBakers — if you just want a different sync provider

If auto-import is the whole point, Wallet connects to 15,000+ institutions with smart categorisation, spending limits, and family sharing, plus solid multi-currency support. Bank sync is the premium feature (~$2.99–5.49/mo), and it runs on iOS, Android, and web — a straight swap for Zenmoney's model with a more polished interface.

### Money Manager (Realbyte) — manual tracking, done thoroughly

Money Manager keeps Zenmoney's report-heavy feel without the bank connection: double-entry-style accounts, main and sub-categories, multi-currency, and weekly/monthly/annual statistics. It's ad-supported free, with a $19.99/yr subscription or a one-time unlock, and runs on iOS, Android, and PC — good for people who like entering everything by hand and studying the charts later.

### YNAB — if the reports were hiding an overspending problem

Detailed reports tell you what happened; YNAB's zero-based method (109 dollars a year) changes what happens next by giving every dollar a job before you spend it. Bank sync is optional. If Zenmoney's reports kept showing the same leaks, read [what zero-based budgeting is](/blog/what-is-zero-based-budgeting) before switching.

## The bottom line

Want automatic import and just need a better-supported provider: Wallet by BudgetBakers. Like entering everything yourself and reading the numbers: Money Manager. Want to stop overspending at the root: YNAB. Want the reports *and* the privacy of never linking a bank — logging fast enough that it actually sticks: Lumi. ### Is Zenmoney free?

There's a free Basic tier, but it's mostly manual. The features Zenmoney is known for — bank sync, planning, and the fuller reports — require the paid Full Zen subscription, available monthly, yearly, or as a lifetime purchase.
